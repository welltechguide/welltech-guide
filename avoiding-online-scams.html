ONLINE SAFETY

How to Avoid Online Scams

Learn how to recognize warning signs and protect yourself when looking for online work, using digital services or doing business on the internet.

Why Online Safety Matters

The internet provides many legitimate opportunities to learn, work and build a business. However, scammers also use online platforms to target people looking for jobs, freelance work, investments, products and business opportunities.

Learning how to recognize suspicious behavior can help you make better decisions before sending money, sharing personal information or accepting an online offer.

Common Online Scams

Online scams can take different forms. Some of the most common examples include:

  • Fake freelance or remote job offers.
  • Requests for money before receiving a job.
  • Fake investment opportunities promising unrealistic returns.
  • Fake clients who send suspicious payment instructions.
  • Phishing messages designed to steal passwords or account information.
  • Fake websites pretending to represent legitimate companies.

Example: A Suspicious Job Offer

Imagine someone contacts you and promises a high-paying online job. Before you can start, they ask you to pay a registration fee, training fee or equipment fee directly to them.

This should make you stop and investigate. A request for upfront payment does not automatically prove that something is a scam, but it is an important warning sign that deserves careful verification.

Warning Signs to Watch For

Be especially careful when an online opportunity contains several of these warning signs:

  • You are promised unusually high earnings with little or no effort.
  • Someone pressures you to make a quick decision.
  • You are asked to send money before receiving meaningful work or services.
  • The person refuses to provide verifiable information about the company or opportunity.
  • Communication is filled with unusual payment instructions or requests for secrecy.
  • You are asked to share passwords, verification codes or other sensitive account information.

Protect Your Accounts

Use strong, unique passwords for important accounts and enable two-factor authentication whenever it is available.

Never give your password or one-time verification code to another person. Legitimate companies generally do not need your private login credentials or verification codes.

Verify Before You Pay

Before sending money to a person or company online, take time to verify who you are dealing with.

Visit the company's official website yourself rather than relying only on links or contact information supplied by a stranger.

Search for independent information about the company and carefully examine the terms of the offer.

Be Careful With Personal Information

Avoid sharing unnecessary personal information with people or websites you do not trust.

Be particularly careful with passwords, banking information, identity documents, authentication codes and other sensitive information.

A Simple Rule

If an opportunity sounds too good to be true, slow down and investigate before taking action.

You do not have to accept an opportunity simply because someone creates urgency around it.

What to Do If You Suspect a Scam

If something appears suspicious, stop communicating until you have verified the situation.

Do not send additional money or sensitive information. Keep relevant messages, receipts and other records that may help you report the incident.

If you believe your account has been compromised, change your password immediately and contact the relevant service provider through its official support channels.

Build Skills and Choose Legitimate Opportunities

One of the best ways to build a sustainable online career is to develop a useful skill and learn how legitimate platforms and businesses operate.

Avoid opportunities that focus mainly on getting money from you instead of providing a clear product, service or source of value.
